Lucid Glass

A vanilla JavaScript & WebGL backdrop-filter to get legible text over any photo.

IPAX-backdrop

A backdrop-filter whose backdrop is corrected to guarantee WCAG 2 contrast, using WebGL or CPU to adjusts the lightness (L in OKLab) of the photo underneath the text, pixel by pixel, only as far as the requested contrast needs.

Where it cannot know what is underneath, it lays a translucent veil computed for the worst case.

The text boxes above are plain HTML elements with --ipax-backdrop-* properties declared in CSS. The H1 additionally applies standard text-shadow; DIV.example-hero-block applies backdrop-filter:blur.

Experiment

Drag any box and resize it from its bottom-right corner. The two boxes with large text use the effect. The others are test backdrops: two photos, a flat colour with alpha, a gradient and a rotated box. The library cannot reproduce the last three and flags them as unknown.

1. Tricontrast keeps the backdrop inside the band where white and black text both reach the ratio. It only exists up to 4.5:1, where monochromatic options narrow just to #757575 and #767676, flattening the picture's luminosity into a near-uniform field.

2. APCA contrast is not offered, as the GPU shader cannot depend on (or faithfully reproduce) the licensed reference implementation.

3. WCAG is defined for text over a plain colour; it says nothing about how the formula should be adjusted for text over a photograph. In my experiments, image detail "fights" the legibility of the text: without blur, it's better to raise the contrast to the next tier (from 3 to 4.5, and from 4.5 to 7). A blurred backdrop moves the image closer to the flat-field case the model measures, so the reported ratio becomes a more meaningful predictor of legibility.
